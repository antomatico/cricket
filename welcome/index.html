Game format: what changes in Year 7

Year 7 is a step up from last season. These are the key rules from Cricket Wellington's Year 7/8 playing rules.

The game

  • 9 players a side, up to 30 overs per team. Games run for about 3 hours.
  • 18m pitch (up from 16m) and boundaries of up to 40m from the centre of the pitch.
  • Hard ball: a red or pink 142g ball (Kookaburra Crown or Menace, or Gray Nicholls Shield).
  • Passing the other team's total doesn't end the game. Everyone still gets their turn.

Batting

  • 6-ball grace period. After a batter has faced 6 balls, all dismissals apply.
  • When a batter is dismissed, they change ends and 5 runs are added to the bowling team's score.
  • Retirement after 30 balls. Wides and no-balls count towards those 30.
  • Coming back in: retired batters can return later with no grace period, in the order they retired. A batter dismissed in their grace period who then faces 30 balls can't return.
  • LBW only if the ball hits the back leg and the batter doesn't offer a shot. No "mankad" run-outs (the bowler can't run out the non-striker for backing up).

Bowling

  • All 9 players bowl 3 overs before anyone bowls a 4th.
  • Maximum of 5 overs per bowler.
  • Overs are 6 balls, with no extra balls for wides or no-balls, except in the final over (8 balls maximum).
  • Run-ups of 15m or less from the stumps.
  • Bowling changes ends every 5 overs.
  • Wides and no-balls follow junior rules: full tosses above the waist, balls that bounce twice before the crease, and balls over the shoulder on the bounce are all called. The official sheet has the wide guidelines.

Fielding

  • Fielders rotate clockwise after each over, so everyone gets a go in different positions.
  • No fielder within 10m of the striking batter or of another fielder. Slips are the only exception.

Helmets are compulsory for all Gladiators when batting and wicketkeeping, at games and at practice. This is a team rule.

Full details are in Cricket Wellington's Year 7/8 Playing Rules.

Player of the Day & Spirit of Cricket

Player of the Day is awarded after every game.

Eastern Suburbs Junior Cricket Club also has a Spirit of Cricket award, given out each game:

  • one to a player from the opposition, and
  • one to a member of our own team.

The Spirit of Cricket award isn't about the most runs or wickets. It recognises kindness, support for teammates, leadership, fair play and looking after the game: clapping in an opposition batter, helping a teammate who's having a tough day, owning up, or setting a great example in the field.

Scoring: we need you!

We record every game's score in PlayHQ, the app Cricket Wellington uses for junior cricket. Each game, we need one parent to be our scorer.

Having a parent score frees Simon and Ants to do the on-field jobs: warming the team up, umpiring and running the match day.

How it works

  • Create a free PlayHQ account at playhq.com/signup.
  • On game day, you score the match ball by ball in the app on your phone. No cricket expertise needed. It's easy to pick up, and we'll help you get started.
  • The opposition has their own scorer too. The two scorers usually sit together to compare notes and agree (or disagree agreeably!) on what happened.

Once you've signed up, please let us know on WhatsApp so we can share the scoring around the team.

Last season in review

Last summer, as the Easts Y6 Gladiators, we played 10 completed T20 games across the Blue grade (Nov–Dec) and Green grade (Jan–Mar).

3Wins
42Wickets
77Fours

What went well

  • 3 wins, including a 51-run win over the BJCC Typhoons and a strong finish, winning our final game of the season against the Easts Spartans.
  • 42 wickets taken (about 4 a game).
  • 77 fours hit across the season.
  • Sharp in the field: 13 catches and 23 run-out involvements. Our fielding and backing up were real strengths.
  • We stayed competitive. Several losses were by 15–16 runs, so we were often only a few good overs away.

What we'll work on this season

  1. Bowling accuracy. This is the big one. We bowled 294 wides (about 29 a game) plus 47 no-balls, and extras made up nearly half the runs we conceded. Tightening our line and length is the fastest way to turn close games into wins.
  2. Scoring off the bat. Extras made up 45% of our own runs, too. More good contact, running hard between the wickets and turning ones into twos will help us post bigger totals ourselves.
  3. Chasing. We won 3 of 6 when batting first, but 0 of 4 when bowling first. We'll practise pacing an innings and knowing what the target needs.
